# Protocol: errors of open-weight models on contracts with known ground truth

Status: **draft v0.2, 1 November 2026, pending review by an independent legal reviewer.** The version used for the runs will be frozen and tagged in this repository before any model is queried. Any change after the first real run will be logged below with its reason, and flagged in the report.

## 1. Question

On fictional contracts whose errors are known in advance, how many errors and hallucinations do mid-sized open-weight models make when asked to summarise, extract clauses and flag risks, and what must a human check before relying on the result?

## 2. Corpus

- **20 fictional contracts in French**, five per type: services agreement, commercial lease, non-disclosure agreement, terms of sale. Generated by `banc/generer_corpus.py` with seed `20261101`: the same script and seed rebuild the same corpus byte for byte (SHA-1 of `corpus/verite.json`: `e7a26df5c01c3c19ebfbc80aae18253f9134eeaa`).
- **Each contract carries** 14 fields, present or absent (parties, amount, start date, duration, notice period, automatic renewal, late-payment penalty, governing law, court, liability cap, security deposit, confidentiality period, non-compete); **decoys** (a revised quote next to the final price, an annual rent next to the monthly rent, a signature date next to the start date); and three to five neutral clauses drawn at random, which bury the important ones.
- **Traps**: 30 in total, across 16 contracts, covering a closed list of 8 risk categories (see `banc/consigne.txt`). **Four control contracts with no trap** (the fifth of each type) measure false alarms.
- **Ground truth**: `corpus/verite.json`, never shown to the models. A readable version for the reviewer is `corpus/fiche_relecteur.md`.
- **Known limit**: a generated corpus is more regular than a real contract. The reviewer judges whether the traps are realistic and the controls truly risk-free; corrections go into the generator, never by hand into the files.

## 3. Models and settings

- **Tier 1, four models** served by Ollama: `qwen3.8:27b`, `qwen3.6:35b`, `phi4:14b`, `gpt-oss:20b`. The exact model digests (`ollama list`) are recorded on the day of the runs and published.
- **Tier 2, if funding allows**: `gpt-oss:120b` or `qwen3.5:122b`.
- **Same settings for all**: same prompt (`banc/consigne.txt`, in French, like the contracts), temperature 0.7, 8,192-token context, a different seed for each run (1, 2, 3). Reasoning mode is left at each model's default and recorded. A non-zero temperature is a deliberate choice: it is what makes run-to-run variation measurable.
- **3 runs** per contract and per model: 4 x 20 x 3 = **240 answers** at tier 1.

## 4. What is measured, exact definitions

Each answer is scored against the ground truth by the published code in `banc/`. An answer that is not readable JSON counts as a **format failure**: all its fields count as omitted and no risk as flagged. The format-failure rate is reported separately.

- **a. Invented clause**: the model gives a value for a field absent from the contract, and that value appears nowhere in the text; or it flags a risk whose quotation cannot be found in the contract. **Rate** = inventions / items asserted by the model (non-null fields + flagged risks).
- **b. Wrong value**: the field exists and the model gives another value; or the field is absent but the model fills it with a value found elsewhere in the text (a decoy, an indemnity read as a price). **Rate** = wrong values / fields present in the ground truth.
- **c. Omission**: a present field returned as null, or a trap not flagged. Two rates: omitted fields / present fields, missed traps / traps.
- **d. False reassurance**: on a contract with at least one trap, the model rates overall risk "none" or "low" **and** misses at least one trap. Counted per answer.
- **Confidence**: share of errors a, b and c that the model marked "high" confidence. This is the core oversight measure: an error announced as uncertain gets caught, an error stated with high confidence slips through.
- **False alarms**: flagged risks, correctly quoted, that match no trap. Counted separately, especially on the control contracts.
- **Variation**: per model, share of (contract, field) pairs whose value changes between runs, and the gap between best and worst run on each rate.
- **Matching rules**: numbers equal within 0.5%; dates in YYYY-MM-DD, exact match; booleans, exact match; text compared without accents or case, every word of the ground truth must appear in the answer. A quotation is "found" if 80% of its words appear, in order, in one line of the contract. A risk detects a trap if it has the same category and a found quotation that shares at least half its words with the trap's text.

## 5. Pass thresholds, fixed before the tests

A model is rated **"usable under human review"** if all four hold over its 60 answers:

- a. zero false reassurance;
- b. less than 5% wrong values;
- c. less than 2% invented clauses;
- d. less than 10% missed traps.

These four numbers are choices made by the author before any run, not values taken from a source. Threshold d was added before any real run, after a simulation showed that a model missing one trap in six passed the first three thresholds as long as it rated the overall risk "high". No model is ever rated "usable without review": the question is what the human must check, not whether the human can be removed.

## 6. Human check of the scoring

A sample of 24 answers (10%, drawn with a published seed) is re-checked by hand against the ground truth. Agreement between automatic scoring and the manual check is published. Below 95% agreement, the scoring code is corrected and everything is re-scored.

## 7. Publication

- **Before the runs**: this protocol, the prompt, the generator and the corpus, in this repository, tagged.
- **After**: all results, including bad ones, the raw model answers, the scoring code, the tables and the report.

## 8. What this protocol does not measure

- The legal quality of advice: the contracts are fictional, the traps are known and the category list is closed.
- Speed and cost on a given machine: compute is rented and not representative of a workstation.
- Closed models: out of scope, the subject is local use of open-weight models.
